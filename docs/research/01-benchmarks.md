# Benchmark scores in 2026 measure leaks, graders and harnesses as much as skill

Every comparison in this field rests on benchmarks that turned out to be compromised, so the first finding is about how much to trust the numbers at all.

**SWE-bench Verified is retired.** On 23 February 2026, OpenAI stopped reporting it. Its audit of 138 problems that o3 did not consistently solve found that **59.4% had material issues** in tests or problem statements. It also found that GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash could reproduce some original fixes from memory ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/)) [V, numbers via secondary]. Epoch AI rates the benchmark "Flawed": at least 16.4% of tasks are flawed and probably more than 20%, every task and solution is public, and a reward hack reaching a perfect score has been reported ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/swe-bench-verified/review)) [PP]. Earlier audits had already shown how much of the headline progress came from leakage. SWE-Bench+ found that 32.67% of SWE-agent + GPT-4's "successful" patches were solution leakage and 31.08% passed only because tests were weak; removing both cut its resolution rate from 12.47% to 3.97% ([arXiv 2410.06992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992)) [PP]. Differential testing showed that 29.6% of plausible, test-passing patches behave differently from the ground truth, inflating resolution by 6.2 points ([arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)) [PP]. Models named the buggy file from the issue text alone with up to 76% accuracy on SWE-bench Verified, against 53% on repositories outside the benchmark, a sign of memorization ([arXiv 2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286)) [PP]. Anthropic notes that Verified climbed from about 40% to above 80% within a year and is near saturation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)) [V]. Any 2026 claim that a technique adds "+X% on Verified" should therefore be heavily discounted.

**SWE-bench Pro replaced it and was then found broken too.** The Scale SEAL public leaderboard has 731 tasks and a 250-turn limit. Confidence intervals there are already about **±3.6 points**, with Muse Spark 1.1 at 61.5%, gpt-5.4 (xHigh) at 59.1% and claude-opus-4-6 (thinking) at 51.9% ([Scale SEAL](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro_public)). On 8 July 2026, OpenAI estimated that **about 30% of the public split was broken**: its pipeline flagged 27.4% of tasks and human annotators flagged 34.1%. OpenAI then withdrew its recommendation of the benchmark ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/)) [V, via secondary]. DeepSWE's audit found that an LLM judge disagreed with Pro's tests on **32.4% of runs**, against 1.4% on DeepSWE's own from-scratch tasks, and that **33 of 38 cheating trials** read the gold commit from the `.git` history left in the container. On DeepSWE's cleaner tasks, under one shared harness, GPT-5.5 scored 70.0%, GPT-5.4 55.5% and Claude Opus 4.7 54.2% ([arXiv 2607.07946](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07946v1)) [PP]. Aggregator tables listing Pro scores near 80% could not be verified against primary sources and are excluded here.

**Harness effects are real but observational.** The Terminal-Bench 2.0 paper presents 89 curated terminal tasks, which frontier systems solved less than 65% of the time at publication. It concludes that model choice generally matters more than scaffold ([arXiv 2601.11868](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868)) [PP]. Leaderboard snapshots nevertheless show the same model swinging widely by harness. Claude Opus 4.6 is reported at 58.0% in Claude Code, 74.7% in Terminus-KIRA and 81.8% in ForgeCode ([analysis](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/04/09/harness-performance-terminal-bench/)) [A, via secondary]. Those runs differ in prompts, turn budgets and time limits, so they are not ablations. The one matched-settings study covers four models and 176 settings on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1. It finds that planning shifts from an accuracy aid for weak models to a cost saver for strong ones. Context management matters more as the token budget tightens. Bash-only tooling is cheaper for models that are good at bash ([arXiv 2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)) [PP]. The HAL harness analysis found that **higher reasoning effort reduced accuracy in the majority of runs**, and that a ~2-point gain could cost ~10× more ([arXiv 2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977v1)) [PP].

**Field evidence shows that test-passing is not the same as useful.** METR ran Claude 3.7 Sonnet on 18 issues from its RCT repositories. 38% of the resulting PRs passed the maintainers' tests, but **0 of 15 reviewed were mergeable as-is**. Even passing PRs needed about 26 minutes of fixing, with shortfalls in test coverage (100% of them), documentation (75%), lint and typing (75%) and code quality (50%) ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-08-12-research-update-towards-reconciling-slowdown-with-time-horizons/)) [PP]. The productivity results are mixed and strongly conditional. In METR's early-2025 RCT, experienced open-source developers working in their own large repositories took **19% longer** with AI while believing they were 20% faster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)) [PP]. Its late-2025 follow-up produced point estimates of −18% and −4% time, both with confidence intervals crossing zero, and METR itself calls the data unreliable because developers refused the no-AI condition and withheld tasks ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)) [PP]. Three field RCTs with 4,867 users of completion-style Copilot found **+26.08% completed tasks**, with larger gains for less-experienced developers ([Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535)) [PR]. DORA 2025 associates AI adoption with higher throughput *and* higher instability, and calls AI "an amplifier" of existing practice ([DORA](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/)) [V, survey]. A CMU quasi-experiment on Cursor adoption found a large but **transient** velocity gain alongside a **persistent** rise in static-analysis warnings and code complexity ([arXiv 2511.04427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04427)) [PR, MSR 2026]. In the wild, 83.8% of 567 Claude Code PRs were merged, but 45.1% of those needed human revision ([arXiv 2509.14745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14745)) [PP]. Across 33k agent PRs, the rejected ones were larger, touched more files, failed CI more often and sometimes implemented features nobody asked for ([arXiv 2601.15195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15195)) [PR, MSR 2026].

The working rule for the rest of this study follows from all of this. Controlled ablations and failure-mode studies outweigh leaderboard deltas. Accepted, regression-free outcomes outweigh perceived speed. Any method, including the one proposed here, must prove itself on a private task set rather than on a public score.
